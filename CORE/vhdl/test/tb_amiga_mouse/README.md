# Amiga mouse benches (issue #190)

`M2M/vhdl/amiga_mouse.vhd` turns the Amiga mouse of one joystick socket into a Commodore 1351 in proportional mode: a synchronizer and a glitch filter per line, a quadrature decoder per axis, a backlog with a rate limiter, the position as POT values with full units only, and the right and middle button derived from the measured paddle values. `M2M/vhdl/debouncer.vhd` conditions the two joystick ports: debouncing, two Amiga mice, the right click with the MEGA key, the port switcher and the on/off switches of the Shell. `M2M/vhdl/m2m_keyb.vhd` delivers the state of the MEGA key for the right click. The settings "Amiga" (mouse), "MEGA+left = right" and "Atari CX30" (paddles) of each port travel from `MEGA65_Core` through the four board files and `framework.vhd` to the debouncer and to `qnice_wrapper.vhd` (paddle measurement), and the four paddle values cross from the QNICE clock to the core clock through `cdc_stable`. The specification is `doc/dev-input/SPEC.md`, sections 3 to 5 (with 5.1), 7 and 8.

## How to run

```
CORE/vhdl/test/tb_amiga_mouse/run.sh [decoder|buttons|ports|keyb|wiring|synth|all] [--keep] [--jobs N] [--no-mutants]
```

* `decoder`: `amiga_mouse` alone (`tb_amiga_mouse.vhd`), the parts `dir`, `double`, `pulse`, `model`, `rate_follow`, `rate_sat`, `rate_tick`, `driver`, `powerup`, `enable` and `times` below, plus the mutants of the decoder.
* `buttons`: the parts `buttons` and `defaults`, plus the mutants of the button logic.
* `ports`: `debouncer` (`tb_amiga_ports.vhd`), the parts `identity`, `modes`, `rule` and `directed`, plus the mutants of the port conditioning and of the right click.
* `keyb`: the MEGA key output of `m2m_keyb` with the keyboard model of `../tb_capslock` and the end-to-end case (`tb_amiga_keyb.vhd`), plus the mutants of `m2m_keyb.vhd`.
* `wiring`: the wiring of the settings and of the paddle values (`wiring.py`), the crossing of the paddle values in simulation (`tb_amiga_cdc.vhd`), the port switcher of the paddle values (`tb_amiga_flip.vhd.in`), plus the mutants of the wiring.
* `synth`: `ghdl synth` of `amiga_mouse`, of `debouncer` and of `m2m_keyb`, the latter two compared with the entities of commit `756677a`.
* `all` (default): everything.
* `--keep`: keeps the build directory (with one log per simulation) and prints its path. `--jobs N`: number of parallel simulations (default 4, or the variable `JOBS`). `--no-mutants`: skips the mutants.

The runner ends with `RESULT: PASS` (exit status 0) or `RESULT: FAIL ...`. Requirements: GHDL 5.1.1 (`/usr/local/bin/ghdl`, or set `GHDL`), python3, git. Runtime of `all` about 35 minutes with 2 processes (`--jobs 2`); every simulation has a timeout of 25 minutes.

## The decoder and the buttons (`tb_amiga_mouse.vhd`)

The bench sees the DUT only through its ports (GHDL 5.1.1 does not resolve external names to signals of the DUT). The main checks of the decoder and the driver run at the real clock frequency, 31,527,778 Hz, and the times also at 32,727,273 Hz (the NTSC core clock) with `G_CLK_FREQ` at the PAL value. With `G_STEP_US = 1` the rate limiter ticks every 31 clock cycles and makes every count of the decoder visible within a microsecond; the parts that check the decoder itself use it, all other parts run with the default generics. The parts of the buttons use small time generics (`G_STEP_US = 10`, `G_CONFIRM_US = 100`, `G_STUCK_MS = 5`, which keep the ratios of 10 ticks to confirm a change), and the part `defaults` checks the default times at a slow clock.

The POT value carries full units only (two counts), so the bench tracks the position that it expects in counts and checks the POT value against `64 + position` with bit 0 cleared.

* Mouse model, one per axis: a phase in counts that a piecewise constant speed profile moves, asynchronous to the clock; the four transitions of a cycle lie at 0, `phase / 90`, 2 and `2 + phase / 90`, so the phase between the two lines is 60 to 120 degrees as the generics say, and the counts per second are exact on average. The lines show the sequences of SPEC.md section 4.2, written into `tb_amiga_mouse_pkg.vhd` from the table of the specification.
* Line drivers: with chatter every edge is a burst that ends at the new level, either the new level followed by 1 to 4 pulses of 50 to 500 ns back to the old level, or a slow edge with 3 to 7 toggles of 10 to 200 ns.
* Checker per axis, at every falling clock edge: every change of the POT value is one unit (mod 128) and never comes faster than one per tick; the value always has the format `pot(7) = position(6)`, `pot(6) = not position(6)`, `pot(5 downto 1) = position(5 downto 1)`, `pot(0) = '0'`; optionally the output lies within the true positions of a time window (follow check), and optionally every count that crosses a unit boundary reaches the output in order and within a bound (delay check).

| Part | Generics | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| `dir` | `G_STEP_US = 1`, and the defaults | start value `0x40`; all four transitions of both sequences in both directions count exactly +1 or -1 on their own axis; the POT value changes on every second count (upwards from an odd to an even position, downwards from an even to an odd one) and is exact after every count; +200 and -260 counts wrap within `0x40` to `0xBE` |
| `double` | `G_STEP_US = 1` | a change of both lines of a pair counts nothing, from every state and at both parities, and the next transitions count as the new state says; 600 random moves of both axes at the same instants (+1, -1, double) |
| `pulse` | `G_STEP_US = 1`, PAL and NTSC clock | 300 pulses per line state (16 states) of 5 ns to 62.7 clock periods, single and overlapping on two lines, at least two clock periods apart: no output changes in any clock cycle; 128 pulses of 64.3 clock periods always pass as a count and its reverse (visible when the first count crosses a unit boundary): the filter time in clock periods, 1.989 to 2.040 us at the PAL clock, 1.916 to 1.965 us at the NTSC clock (the simulation has no metastability; on the FPGA the first synchronizer stage may take a new value one clock cycle late, so a level needs 65 periods to pass for certain) |
| `model` | `G_STEP_US = 1`, phases 60/120, 90/75, 120/100 | both axes at the same time with independent random profiles from 3 to 20,000 counts per second, both directions, pauses and single steps, chatter on every edge: in every clock cycle the output lies within the true positions of the last 10 us, and after each run it is exactly the true position |

| `defaults` | 100 kHz clock, default time generics | one count per 400 us, a press is reported 4.0 to 4.4 ms after it began and released after exactly 5.000 s |

## The port conditioning (`tb_amiga_ports.vhd`)

Beside the DUT run three entities that `run.sh` takes from git and renames: `debouncer_ref`, the entity of commit `6845596` (before the Amiga mice), `debouncer_prev`, the entity of commit `756677a` (before the right click with the MEGA key), and a second `debouncer_ref` with the port switcher at '0' and both ports on, whose outputs are the debounced lines of each socket (the fire line of a socket is f of SPEC.md section 5.1). Two instances of `amiga_mouse` with the generics of the DUT get the raw lines and the true measured values of each socket. The paddle inputs of the DUT are what the core delivers: switched to C64 ports and inverted. The outputs are compared 1 ps after every event of an input, of an output of the DUT or of a reference, and 1 ps after every clock edge, so a difference of a single clock cycle cannot pass. Each comparison applies these checks:

* While `amiga_i` is "00": the ten joystick outputs are those of `debouncer_ref` and the four paddle outputs are the paddle inputs, whatever `amiga_rclick_i` and `mega_key_n_i` do.
* While `amiga_rclick_i` is "00": all fourteen outputs are those of `debouncer_prev`.
* The model (`G_MODEL`, on by default), written from the formulas of SPEC.md sections 5 and 5.1, not from the RTL: a flip-flop `mq` per socket that takes "Amiga and switch on and key held" at every rising clock edge at which f is '1', and r = `mq` and Amiga and switch. A C64 port that is off shows five '1'. A C64 port connected to a socket in Amiga mode shows fire = f or r, up = the right button of the reference mouse and (f or not r), down = its middle button, left and right '1', and the inverted position of the reference mouse as paddle values. A C64 port connected to a socket in C64 mode shows what `debouncer_ref` shows.
* The two properties of section 5.1, always, on every C64 port that is on and connected to a socket in Amiga mode whose f is '0': a press is classified when f falls, from the key and the settings as they are at that rising clock edge, and is a right click while that classification holds and the socket is still in Amiga mode with the switch on. During a right click the fire line is never '0'; during a left click the up line is '0' only if the reference mouse reports its right button.

`G_MODEL=false` and `G_IDENT=false` switch the comparisons off, so that a mutant can show what the properties catch on their own. `G_FREQ` is `CLK_FREQ` of all instances; the parts `rule` and `directed` run with a tenth of the PAL frequency (`-gG_FREQ=3152778`) at the real clock period, so that the debouncer and the mice count a tenth of the clock cycles and the bench scales all its times with it; one run of each also uses the real frequency.

| Part | What it proves |
|---|---|
| `identity` | `amiga_i` at "00" and 300 ms of random activity on the ten lines (mostly long enough for the 1 ms debouncer), the port switcher, the on/off inputs, resets, the paddle values, both switches and the key: the outputs are those of `debouncer_ref` and the paddle inputs at every comparison |
| `modes` | all 32 combinations of `flip_joys_i`, `amiga_i(0)`, `amiga_i(1)`, `joy_1_on` and `joy_2_on` for 12 to 16 ms each, then 30 random switches during activity, both switches off and the key toggling at random: the outputs are those of `debouncer_prev` and of the model. A socket in Amiga mode gets quadrature on both pairs, the left button on fire and right and middle button presses as measured values; a socket in C64 mode gets random joystick lines and paddle values. The quadrature of an Amiga socket therefore never reaches a direction output. The reference mice have to change their buttons and positions often enough, or the bench fails |
| `rule` | 200 episodes at a tenth of the frequency (seeds 1 and 2) and 80 at the real frequency (seed 3). Each episode sets random settings (mostly Amiga mode with the switch on), the port switcher, the on/off switches and the key, and is then one of four kinds, which take turns: a press of socket 0 alone; a press of socket 1 alone; a press of both sockets, pressed 0 to 4 clock cycles apart and released 0 to 4 clock cycles apart, with the socket that is pressed first and the one that is released first going through all four combinations in turn; or one socket held for a whole press of the other, the held socket alternating: it is pressed, the key changes at least once, the other socket is pressed and released, the key keeps changing, and the held socket is released. In every second turn the single presses are released and pressed again as early as the debouncer allows (the raw line goes low at the falling clock edge before the rising edge at which f rises, so that f stays '1' for C_DB + 2 clock cycles, 1.000 ms at the real frequency), held and released again. Around every fall and every rise of f that a press or a release of the bench causes (the edges that a reset causes get none): one key change in one of the clock cycles c(-3) to c(2), chosen by a rotation per socket so that the six are hit in turn, with a probability of 0.5 a second key change in a random one of them, and a change of the switch (with a probability of 0.2 of the Amiga setting instead) of that socket in a clock cycle of a second rotation. While buttons are held: key changes, rarely a change of a setting, of the port switcher or of on/off, and a rare short reset, which ends the presses and lets the held buttons start new ones about 1 ms later; the right buttons of the reference mice toggle on their own. c(0) is the clock cycle that begins with the rising edge at which f changes. The bench predicts the edge at which f falls after each press of its own and checks the prediction. From what it observes it counts per socket: the key changes and the setting changes in each of c(-1), c(0) and c(1) around the falls and around the rises of f (twelve numbers), the presses that began as right clicks and as left clicks, how often the socket was pressed first and released first when both were pressed, how often it was held for a whole press of the other without a reset in that episode, how often it was pressed again as early as possible, the shortest time that f stayed '1' before a fall and how many falls came after exactly that time. The shortest time has to be C_DB + 2 clock cycles, and each count has to reach a floor that grows with the number of episodes and is never below 4: the key changes per clock cycle `G_EPISODES`/10, the setting changes per clock cycle and the right clicks `G_EPISODES`/16, the left clicks `G_EPISODES`/4, both orders, the presses again at the shortest distance and the falls after it `G_EPISODES`/10, the long presses without a reset `G_EPISODES`/16. The job `mutant_rule_starved` (6 episodes, must fail) shows that the check can fail |
| `directed` | the cases of section 5.1, each with explicit checks of the up and the fire line (135 checks): power-up with both switches on; key first, then the button, with the key released and pressed again during the press (a right click throughout); button first, then the key (a left click throughout); the key released and pressed in each of c(-2) to c(2) of the fall of f, checked in c(0) and later (a change in c(-1) decides, one in c(0) does not); the right button of the mouse before, during and after a right click, also while the key is held without a press; both sockets at once with different switches, and one press with and one without the key; socket 1 held after a press without the key, the key pressed, then socket 0 pressed (a right click, while socket 1 stays a left click), and socket 1 held after a press with the key, the key released, then socket 0 pressed (a left click, while socket 1 stays a right click), and the same with the sockets exchanged; for both sockets, a right click, the key released while the button is held, the button released and pressed again as early as the debouncer allows (a left click, checked in c(0) of the new press and later, with f '1' for exactly C_DB + 2 clock cycles in between), and the same from a left click with the key pressed (a right click); for both sockets, a reset of the debouncers during a right click with the button held throughout (no line pressed during the reset and until the debouncer reports the held button again about 1 ms after it; then a left click if the key was released during the reset, a right click if it stayed pressed, as the formulas of section 5.1 give with f forced to '1' by the reset); a socket in C64 mode with the switch on (a joystick fire stays a fire); the switch and the Amiga setting changing during a press, and switched on during a press that began without them (a left click stays a left click); through the port switcher and with a port switched off |

## The MEGA key (`tb_amiga_keyb.vhd`)

The DUT is the keyboard chain `m2m_keyb` → `mega65kbd_to_matrix` → `matrix_to_keynum` (with `kb_matrix_ram`) at the PAL core clock, with the behavioral MEGA65 keyboard of `../tb_capslock/tb_capslock_keyb.vhd` in front of it: after each sync pulse one bit per `kio8` clock on `kio10`, bit n = key n, low active, bit 72 = Caps Lock. The bench keeps the model in a copy of its own, so that the keyboard parts of the two benches stay independent.

| Run | What it proves |
|---|---|
| `keyb` | beside the DUT runs `m2m_keyb_prev`, the entity of commit `756677a`, with the same inputs; at every event of an output and at every falling clock edge `kio8_o`, `kio9_o`, `key_num_o`, `key_pressed_n_o`, `capslock_n_o` and `qnice_keys_n_o` are identical (Caps Lock and the LED inputs change now and then, so that all of them move). At every rising clock edge: `mega_key_n_o` is '0' or '1', it changes only at an edge that ends a clock cycle in which `key_num_o` is 61, at such an edge with `enable_core_i` at '1' it takes the value of `key_pressed_n_o` of that cycle, and before the first such edge it is '1'; while `enable_core_i` is '0', `key_pressed_n_o` is '1'. The MEGA key pressed and released 24 times at random phases, alternately with `enable_core_i` at '1' and at '0': `mega_key_n_o` follows a press within 1.6 ms and a release within 2.7 ms (the runner prints the measured delays). The bounds follow from the chain: a change reaches the matrix within one keyboard frame (141 x 102 clock cycles, 0.456 ms), and `matrix_to_keynum` reports a press at the next visit of the scan (one scan: 80 x 438 clock cycles, 1.111 ms) and a release only at a visit after a whole scan without the key, so a press takes at most 1.567 ms and a release 2.678 ms and `key_pressed_n_o` stays '1' while the core is decoupled. Each of the other 79 key bits alone for 3 ms: `mega_key_n_o` stays '1', and the scan reports keys 2 to 79 (bits 0 and 1 of the frame are not stored by `mega65kbd_to_matrix`); all 79 together, with the core coupled and decoupled: `mega_key_n_o` stays '1' and still follows the MEGA key |
| `keyb_start` | the same with the MEGA key held from power-up: `mega_key_n_o` is '1' until the first visit of the scan at key 61 and '0' from the rising clock edge that ends the first clock cycle of that visit (0.847 ms after power-up, one clock cycle after `key_num_o` turns 61; a visit lasts 438 clock cycles) |
| `keyb_e2e` | the real `m2m_keyb` and the real `debouncer` connected as in `framework.vhd` (`mega_key_n_o` to `mega_key_n_i`, one clock), socket 0 in Amiga mode with the switch on, the paddle inputs of a mouse without pull-ups: the MEGA key pressed in the keyboard model, then the fire pin of socket 0 low: the up line of C64 port 1 goes low and the fire line stays '1' in every clock cycle, also after the key is released during the press; a press without the key is a left click |

## The wiring (`wiring.py`, `tb_amiga_cdc.vhd`)

`wiring.py check` works on the files of the working tree and on those of the base commit `6845596`:

* GHDL: the four board files analyze and elaborate against shells (the real entity declarations with empty architectures) of `framework`, `MEGA65_Core`, `audio`, `max10` and `pcm_to_pdm`. `framework.vhd`, `qnice_wrapper.vhd` and the four board files are analyzed in the version of the base commit and of the working tree, each in a library with what it instantiates (the real `debouncer`, `amiga_mouse`, `cdc_stable` and `mouse_input`, shells of `qnice_wrapper` and `m2m_keyb`, shells for the board files) and with a stub of the Xilinx library `xpm` that declares the component `xpm_cdc_array_single` as Vivado does, so that GHDL checks the port maps of `i_sys2main`, `i_main2qnice` and `i_qnice2main`, the widths of the actuals included. The working tree must not show a diagnostic that the base does not show, and only "not found" for the entities that are not in the library.
* Static checks, for what GHDL cannot see: in `i_qnice2main` every `src_in` slice has a `dest_out` slice with the identical range, the slices are contiguous from 0, do not overlap and add up to `WIDTH`, and each source is paired with the destination it had in the base commit, except the four paddle values, which are gone. `i_qnice2main_paddles` is a `cdc_stable` with 32 bits and a source register from `qnice_clk` to `main_clk_i`; its byte k carries the k-th paddle value of the base, and its destination feeds the input of `i_joy_debouncer` whose output is the destination that value had in the base. `i_joy_debouncer` gets `main_mouse_amiga_i` and, as `flip_joys_i`, the destination of `qnice_flip_joyports_i` in `i_qnice2main` (the core clock domain version), `main_pot1_x_o` to `main_pot2_y_o` have no other source, `i_qnice_wrapper` gets `qnice_paddle_atari_i`, `qnice_wrapper.vhd` passes bit 0 to `atari_paddles_a` and bit 1 to `atari_paddles_b`. The right click: `framework` has the port `main_mouse_rclick_i : in std_logic_vector(1 downto 0)`, `i_joy_debouncer` gets `amiga_rclick_i => main_mouse_rclick_i` as a whole (no slices), and `mega_key_n_o` of `i_m2m_keyb` reaches `mega_key_n_i` of `i_joy_debouncer` through a signal of type `std_logic` that appears nowhere else in `framework.vhd` (the MEGA key is not exported). Each board file connects the three outputs `qnice_paddle_atari_o`, `main_mouse_amiga_o` and `main_mouse_rclick_o` of `MEGA65_Core` to the three inputs of `framework` through three distinct signals of `std_logic_vector(1 downto 0)`, each used exactly twice.
* Project files: every entity that `framework.vhd`, `qnice_wrapper.vhd`, `debouncer.vhd`, `amiga_mouse.vhd`, `mega65.vhd` and the board file instantiate (a VHDL entity or a Verilog module) is declared in a source file of each of `CORE/CORE-R3.xpr` to `CORE-R6.xpr`, unless it was missing there in the base commit already (`sdram` in `CORE-R3.xpr`, in the generate branch of `framework.vhd` that the R3 board does not elaborate).

`tb_amiga_cdc.vhd` simulates the crossing with two unrelated clocks, 50 MHz and 31,527,778 Hz. A source register takes 100,000 random values for the four paddle bytes at random intervals of 160 ns to 1.5 us (the measuring cycle is 514 us; what matters for the crossing is that each value holds for more than a few clock periods of the destination); between the register and the destination every bit has its own routing delay of 0 to 3 ns. The path of `framework.vhd`, `cdc_stable` followed by `debouncer` with its two mice, must show every source value at the paddle outputs, in order, and never a value that the source did not hold. The same delayed bits pass a model of the per-bit synchronizers of `xpm_cdc_array_single` (two flip-flops per bit; a bit that changes within 0.2 ns before an edge is sampled with a random value), which must show torn values for the comparison.

`tb_amiga_flip.vhd.in` is the template of a bench into which `run.sh` copies the process `correct_and_flip_paddles` of `qnice_wrapper.vhd` verbatim: in the QNICE clock domain it inverts the four measured values of the two sockets and switches them to the C64 ports. Behind it come the crossings of `framework.vhd` (`cdc_stable` in its byte order, two flip-flops for "Flip joystick ports") and the debouncer with its two mice. With `amiga_i` at "00" and 200 random values in each flip state, the paddle outputs of every C64 port are the inverted values of the socket connected to it. With `amiga_i` at "11" each mouse reports the buttons of its own socket on the C64 port that the socket is connected to, in both flip states, for two button patterns that differ between the sockets, and also 2 us after every change of the flip: no mouse changes its state while the two crossings deliver the flip and the values with different delays.

## Synthesis

`ghdl synth` of `amiga_mouse` (`G_CLK_FREQ = 31,527,778`), of `debouncer` and of `m2m_keyb` must run without an error and without a latch; the runner prints the number of flip-flops of the netlist (141 for one `amiga_mouse`; Vivado removes the constant bit 0 of the POT registers and may merge the POT bits with the position bits they copy). `debouncer` must have exactly two flip-flops more than the entity of `756677a` (464 and 462), `m2m_keyb` exactly one of its own more (42 and 41). GHDL does not synthesize the real sub-entities of `m2m_keyb` (an integer port of range 0 to 9 on a formal of range 0 to 15 in `mega65kbd_to_matrix`), so `keyb_synth_stubs.vhd` stands in for them and only the flip-flops of `m2m_keyb` itself are compared. GHDL ignores the attribute `async_reg`; the runner checks that both synchronizer stages carry it.

## Mutants

Each mutant is one exact text replacement in a copy of a source (`mutants.py`); the named bench part runs it with `G_STOP` and must not end with `TB: RESULT PASS`.

| Mutant | Change | Rejected by |
|---|---|---|
| `x_direction_swapped`, `y_direction_swapped` | the two lines of a pair exchanged in the decoder | `dir` |
| `pairs_exchanged` | X decoded from (left, up), Y from (right, down) | `dir` |
| `pot_plus_64_wrong` | the POT value without the +64 mapping | `dir` |
| `pot_bit0_exported` | bit 0 of the position in the POT value | `driver` with the output checkers off: the exactness check of the driver |
| `double_transition_counted` | a change of both lines counts | `double` |
| `filter_removed`, `filter_half`, `filter_one_cycle_longer` | no filter, half the filter time, one clock period more | `pulse` |
| `one_synchronizer_stage` | one synchronizer stage instead of two | expected to survive: it only shortens the latency by one clock cycle, which no port shows, and removes the protection against metastability, which no simulation sees |
