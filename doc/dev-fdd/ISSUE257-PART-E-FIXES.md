## 1. READY while the head moves

**What the tester saw.** Under JiffyDOS, MFCWT 1.6 (Multi Floppy Commander with Turbo, a cartridge) reported `REREAD DIRECTORY FIRST` or `REREAD TARGET DIRECTORY` at almost every check before a format or a file copy, and the error channel read `74,DRIVE NOT READY` (analysis section 2).

**The cause.** MFCWT sends `UI` before every check, and the DOS recalibrates the head inside that command: a Restore of 39 steps, then a Seek of 39 steps back to the directory cylinder. READY (`media_ready`) fell when no index edge had arrived for 500 ms (`2 * G_PERIOD_MAX_CYC`) while the motor was on. The mechanism of the tester's R6 most likely delivers no index pulses while it steps (inferred from the timing of the failures, not observed). Such mechanisms exist: the TEAC FD-235HF-C929, -C940 and -C991 are delivered with a "ready and seek-complete gate (full-mask) for INDEX and READ DATA" and output INDEX only in the ready state and in the seek-complete state, which begins 15.8 to 17.9 ms after the trailing edge of the final STEP pulse; READ DATA is gated in the same way (TEAC, "FD-235HF-C929/C940/C991 Micro Floppy Disk Drive Specification", Rev. A, sections 3, 8.3.9 and 8.3.10). The document does not say which other FD-235HF variants have the gate. The two trains then hid two index pulses in a row, READY was low for some tens of milliseconds after the Seek, and the DOS examines READY once per job and does not retry. A genuine 1581 passes the same sequence: the READY of its mechanism does not depend on the head moving.

**The rule as built** (`physical_1581_controller.vhd`, the readiness part of the main process; its comment states the rule and its reason):

1. READY rises in the clock after the first index edge of a motor-on interval, also when that edge arrives while the head steps.
2. READY falls with the motor request, on reset, on disable, and when no index edge arrived for 500 ms of settled time.
3. Settled time is every clock in which the step engine is idle (`step_st = SI_IDLE`) and the head has settled (`head_settled = '1'`). `head_settled` falls when a step is accepted and rises `G_SETTLE_CYC` (18 ms) after the trailing edge of that step's pulse. In every other clock `idx_gap_cnt` is held at zero, so the 500 ms start anew when the head has settled.
4. An index edge restarts the count and sets `idx_seen` whenever it arrives, during a step as well.
5. Unchanged: the disk-change latch, the conditions of the read engine, of the write engine and of the read-ahead that use `media_ready`, and the spin-up measurement.

Both terms of rule 3 are needed. When `G_SETTLE_CYC` is shorter than the recovery time of a step, `head_settled` rises during the recovery, and only the term of the step engine holds the count there; the benches run with both ratios (section 8).

**Why it is safe.**

* A disk that is taken out while the head moves is noticed 500 ms after the head has settled instead of 500 ms after its last index pulse. In between, the disk-change line guards the data as it did. Its latch is set by the raw line and cleared only by a completed step with the line released, so steps without a disk cannot clear it. The DOS's readiness stage looks at the change line first and ends the job with 74 after its step pair, as under the old rule; the exam of READY before a Read Address comes only after that stage. The read engine aborts on the level of the disk-change line in every state, so a read in the window ends at once with "disk changed" and without a byte. The write engine's change term fires once, in a write that runs when the line asserts; a pull during a train fires it while no write runs, so a write issued later does not see it. Such a later write is guarded twice: the DOS looks at the latch (CIA PA7) at the start of every job that uses the disk and ends the job with 74 before it writes, and without a disk there is no CRC-valid sector header for a Write Sector and no index edge for a Write Track, while the gate opens only behind such a header or at such an edge; a Write Sector in the window ends when READY falls. The bench shows both in the window (section 8.1).
* A disk that is exchanged during a train has its change latch cleared by the train's own steps, as on a genuine 1581.
* Without a disk there are no index edges, so READY can stay high only if it was high when the disk left; the guards above cover that case.
* The limit of the rule: on a mechanism that hides its index pulses, READY stays up as long as the hold time plus one revolution is shorter than 500 ms plus the settle time, about 318 ms (320, 319 and 318 ms at 3.03, 6 and 12 ms per step, computed with the rule of the harness for index phases in steps of 2 ms). The seek-complete time of the TEAC document, at most 17.9 ms after the trailing edge of the final STEP pulse, ends before the settle time of the controller, 18 ms after it.

**What it does not do.**

* It does not bound the suspension (D2): steps without end, a disk in the drive or not, keep READY at its last value for as long as they come closer than 500 ms plus the settle time.
* It does not notice a disk that is pulled during a seek before the head has settled, and a mechanism whose hold time exceeds about 318 ms would still lose READY.
* It does not change what READY needs to rise, the first index edge. A first index pulse later than the exam of the ROM (0.800 s after motor-on for an unchanged disk, 0.830 s after a disk change) still fails the first job: that is the limit of the ROM itself (`doc/dev-fdd/ISSUE257.md`, section 2).

## 2. The step rate

**What the tester saw.** After every long seek on the R6's mechanism the head was 2 cylinders short of where the DOS wanted it: the Read Address after a recalibration found cylinder 37 with the track register at 39, and the DOS corrected it with a Seek of two steps and a second Read Address (analysis section 3). Single steps and moves of two steps never slipped, and the data were never at risk.

**The cause.** In physical mode the seek machine of `fdc1772.v` requested one step from the controller and went on with the next one as soon as the controller acknowledged it. The acknowledge comes after the direction setup (24 µs), the pulse (4 µs) and the recovery (`G_STEP_REC_CYC`, 3 ms; `G_STEP_REV_CYC`, 4 ms after a reversal) of the controller's step engine: 3.03 ms per step, 4.03 ms after a reversal, whatever the rate bits of the command said. The 1581 ROM issues Restore `$08` (`$CB0F`), Seek `$18` (`$CE86`), Step In `$48` and Step Out `$68` (`$CD8A`, `$CD93`); its command table at `$C30C` to `$C340` also holds Step `$28`, which it never issues. Rate bits 00, which a WD1772 turns into 6 ms per step. So the head stepped twice as fast as on a 1581, at the limit for which mechanisms of this kind are specified, and the R6's mechanism lost two cylinders in a long train, in either direction. That the pace is the reason is inferred: it is what a stepper motor does that drops one electrical cycle.

**The rule as built** (`fdc1772.v`; everything is behind `phys_mode` or acts on `phys_rate_cnt`, which only the physical branch loads):

1. When the seek machine requests a step in physical mode, `phys_rate_cnt` is loaded with the rate time of the command's bits r1 r0 (`phys_rate_ticks`, 20 bits): 6, 12, 2 or 3 ms for a WD1772, that is 48000, 96000, 16000 or 24000 ticks of the 8 MHz clock enable. It is loaded two ticks short, so that the next request comes exactly one rate time after this one.
2. While `phys_rate_cnt` is not 0, the Type I machine does nothing: no evaluation of track 0 or of the target, no next step, no verify, no end of the command. The count runs out one tick before the rate time has passed. A Seek or Restore spends that tick in state 0, so its next step and its verify come exactly one rate time after the step request and its end one tick later; a Step, Step In or Step Out ends one rate time after its request, but with V = 1 it requests its verify in that tick, 125 ns before the rate time has passed (D19).
3. The acknowledge of the controller stays the lower bound: the machine goes on only when the count has run out and the step is acknowledged. The pace is the larger of the two times: 6 ms for the codes of the ROM (12 ms if its probe of the CIA TOD clock at `$C36A` fails and its command table becomes `$09`, `$19`, `$29`, `$49`, `$69`; in the core the TOD input of the 8520 is `ph2_f`, so the probe passes), and the acknowledge for the codes 10 and 11 (3.03 ms, 4.03 ms after a reversal).
4. BUSY of a command with n steps lasts the sum of its n paces plus two ticks of the 8 MHz clock enable (Step, Step In, Step Out) or three (Seek, Restore). BUSY of a command without a step is the minimum busy time `PHYS_T1_MIN_TICKS`, 1.5 ms, as before.
5. Every accepted command clears `phys_rate_cnt`, and in physical mode it starts the Type I machine in state 0 (D7).
6. Unchanged: the image path (its table `step_rate_clk` and its handling of `seek_state`), the track register, the Restore bound of 255 steps, the verify option, and what a Force Interrupt does to a running command.

What it means for the DOS: a Seek over 39 cylinders takes 234 ms instead of 119 ms, one over 50 cylinders 300 ms, one across the disk (79 cylinders) 474 ms. The step pair of the readiness stage after a disk change takes 12 ms instead of 7.06 ms (3.03 and 4.03 ms: the last step before it went inward, so only the Step Out is a reversal), so the exam of READY after a disk change comes 0.8303 s after motor-on instead of 0.8253 s (proofs I6 and I1).

**Why it is safe.**

* Nothing changes for disk images: the image branch is untouched, its golden trace is byte-identical, and the golden trace rejects a mutant that paces the image path as well.
* The settling of the head is as before. The controller's read and write engines wait for `head_settled` (18 ms after the last pulse), and after every Type I command the DOS waits 18 ms itself (the delay at `$CBD5`, called at `$CB1E` after a Restore, at `$CD9C` after the step pair and at `$CE9C` after a Seek), so BUSY that ends about 6 ms after the last pulse changes nothing.
* Track 0 is examined after the rate time: a Restore whose track 0 sensor answers between the acknowledge and the end of the rate time ends with the right number of steps.
* The format does not become slower: the Write Track after the Seek of one step waits for its index pulse anyway (one cylinder takes 1200.0 ms with the read-ahead, with the steps at the rate and at the acknowledge alike, in the genuine-ROM emulation; section 6).
* At 6 ms per step a long seek lasts longer than the 500 ms that READY tolerated without an index pulse before change 1; hence D15.

**What it does not do.**

* Whether the R6's mechanism stops losing two cylinders at 6 ms can only be shown on the hardware (section 9).
* It does not close the corner in which the controller loses a step request after a Force Interrupt (section 6).
* The image path keeps its own table, which wraps for 12 ms and above (section 6).
* The verify option, which the ROM never sets, verifies 18 ms after the last pulse (the controller's settle time, after the rate time), where a WD1772 waits the rate time plus 15 ms. Of a Step with V = 1 the verify could come one tick before the rate time has passed (rule 2), which the settle time of the controller hides.

## 3. The decoder's tolerance for a weak first sync byte

**What the tester saw.** One format from DraCopy under JiffyDOS stopped after track 78 with error 20: the verify of cylinder 77, side H=1, did not find sector 7 (analysis section 4).

**The cause.** In that pass the first of the three sync bytes in front of the ID of sector 7 was recorded weakly: its flux intervals were 6.47, 6.88, 6.75, 7.80 and 5.60 µs where 6, 8, 6, 8 and 6 µs are nominal (peak shift), the same in every revolution of the capture. The quantiser classifies every interval alone against the midpoints at 2.5 and 3.5 times its estimate of the half cell. The long interval of 6.88 µs fell below the midpoint between "medium" and "long", the candidate of that byte was lost, and a mark needed three candidates at the exact spacing. The header was invisible in every revolution, and the verify of a format has no retry. A WD1772 times every transition against a recovered clock and would most likely have read the header (a judgment: nobody read that track on a 1581).

**The rule as built** (variant A, D9; `physical_1581_mfm_decoder.vhd`, whose header states it):

1. While the parser is idle and `tol_off_i` is low, the decoder accepts the byte after a train of two sync candidates as the mark: two candidates (the second and the third sync byte) at the exact spacing of five gaps, each with a valid complete-word span.
2. `FE` opens an ID. Such an ID is reported only with a good CRC. With a bad CRC it is not reported (no `id_valid_o`), and the arm of the data mark and N of the last ID stay as they were. It does occupy the parser for its six bytes and its CRC check: the limit below.
3. `FB` or `F8` opens a data field only when a CRC-valid ID armed it and the zero run ended 7 to 11 gaps (`C_QUANT_SYNC_LAT + 1` to `C_QUANT_SYNC_LAT + C_QUANT_A1_SPACING`) before the first candidate of the pair. The window of a train of three (at most `C_QUANT_SYNC_LAT` = 6 gaps), moved by the five gaps of the lost sync byte, would be 5 to 11; its lower bound is raised to 7, because a pair that close to the zero run holds the first two sync bytes of a train, and the byte after it is the third sync byte, weak, never the mark.
4. The CRC engine receives the `A1` of the lost byte before the mark, and the mark two clocks later (the engine buffers one byte), so the field is checked exactly as after three sync bytes.
5. A train of two never re-anchors an open field; the re-anchor of the parser on a qualified ID mark stays a matter of trains of three.
6. A weak second or third sync byte is not tolerated (D11).
7. `tol_hit_o` pulses once per mark accepted on a train of two, also for an ID on a pair that then has a bad CRC. `a1_candidate_o`, `a1_span_reject_o`, `a1_train_o`, `mark_fe_o` and `mark_dam_o` keep their meaning (trains of three only) and are identical with and without the tolerance. `dam_unarmed_o` keeps its definition (a data mark of a train of three that the record grammar rejects) and differs where the parser differs: the data mark after an ID recovered on a pair is armed and does not pulse, and a data mark that arrives while an ID opened on a pair is read is ignored and pulses (the limit below). `locked_o` rises also when a mark is accepted on a pair.
8. With `tol_off_i` high the decoder is the decoder without the tolerance, on every output and in every clock. The tolerance exists only with the production values of the sync generics (`C_TOL`, D12).
9. `tol_off_i` comes from bit 0 of `RD_CTRL` of the diagnostic device (`dec_tol_off_o` of the device, `dec_tol_off_i` of the controller); it is 0 after the device's reset, so the tolerance is on.

**Why it is safe.**

* On the twelve flux captures of the issue it changes nothing but the ID and the data field of that sector (section 8): no field lost, none changed, no ID or data field with a bad CRC. The gained ID and data field have a good CRC in all three revolutions, and the Greaseweazle software's own read of that side reports all ten sectors: that is the strong evidence. A PLL decoder of the same kind (`pllcheck.py`) reads the three IDs at the phase gains 0.30 and 0.45, not at its default of 0.60, and the data fields at all three; the two lower gains were chosen on the sides where the tolerance acted, so this confirmation is not independent of the case.
* It does not reopen what the three-byte rule closed. That rule exists against false syncs at write splices. The captures hold 9,894 sectors that the core rewrote one at a time, with their splices. In all twelve captures there are four trains of two: the three of the weak header, and in one revolution the first two sync bytes of the track information mark (`A1 A1 A1 65`) of cylinder 36, H=0, of the 11-sector disk G3-after-C, whose third sync byte is weak (the byte after the pair is `A3`, three or more bits away from `FE`, `FB` and `F8`). So the captures contain no false train of two at all. Splice junk produces isolated candidates, never two at the exact spacing.
* A false `FE` on a pair can neither end a Read Address with an ID CRC error nor disarm the data mark of the ID before it: its ID is not reported unless its CRC is good (a chance of 1 in 65,536 for junk). It does occupy the parser (the limit below).
* A data mark on a pair needs a CRC-valid ID and the lock-up run in front of it, as a data mark after three sync bytes does.
* The CRC covers three `A1` as before, so the drive CPU still never sees a byte of a sector whose CRC was not verified.

**What it does not do, and the residual risks** (a judgment):

* It does not change the quantiser: an interval near a midpoint is still classified alone (variant B was rejected, D9), and a weak second or third sync byte still hides its field.
* **The limit (D18; scenario 20 of the unit bench).** An ID opened on a false pair occupies the parser for the six bytes after its `FE` and its CRC check. A data mark of a train of three among those six bytes is ignored (`dam_unarmed_o`), and that data field is lost in this revolution, where the decoder without the tolerance reads it. In front of a data field this takes a false `A1 A1 FE` inside the field's zero run with three to five zero bytes left between that `FE` and the sync train; with fewer, the run in front of the train is too short for any decoder, and with six or more the false ID ends in time. Every format and every Write Sector writes those zero bytes in the same pass as the sync train. Dropouts or extra pulses in a zero run, which lies on a grid of two half cells, cannot form long, medium, long, medium, and a Write Sector writes its whole zero run and train after the ID, so no old train survives in front of a new one (rewritten data fields start 705 to 709 half cells after their ID instead of 704). No capture contains the pattern. A real ID behind a false pair is not affected: its train of three re-anchors the parser.
* A disk from another drive whose write splices produce two clean candidates at the exact spacing followed by `FE` is the case the captures do not contain. From the captures, with no false train of two in 5,044 side revolutions (95 % bounds, rule of three): at most about 6 in 10,000 revolutions with a train of two from junk, about 2 in a million with a false `FE` on a pair, about 1 in 100 million with a data field lost to the limit above (three positions of the `FE` in front of each of ten data fields, out of 6,250 byte times per revolution), and about 4 in 100 billion with a false ID reported. These bounds take junk to occur anywhere, the zero runs included, which by the point above it cannot.
* Two changes of behavior that are correct: a weak first sync byte in front of a damaged data field gives a data CRC error at once (DOS error 23) instead of "record not found" after five index edges, and the DOS retries both; a Write Sector to a sector whose header has a weak first sync byte and a good CRC succeeds, and its write gate opens where it would after three sync bytes, because `id_valid_o`, `data_start_o` and `data_end_o` of a field accepted on a pair come in the same clock as after three sync bytes (scenario 22 of the unit bench).

## 4. Instruments: diagnostic map v10

| Word | Name | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| `0xA2` | `IDX_GAP_MAX_MS` | the longest time between two index edges in a row with the motor-on level high in every clock between them, in whole milliseconds of real time (truncated) of the clocks strictly between the two edges (the convention of `SPINUP_LAST_MS`), the largest since reset; `0x0000` before the first measurement; saturates at `0xFFFF`. A measurement starts with a motor-qualified index edge (`diag_index_qual_i`) and ends with the next one, which starts the next measurement; a clock with the motor-on level (`st_motor_on_i`, low also in reset and while the drive is disabled) low ends it without a result. It does not depend on READY. About 200 (one revolution) when every index pulse arrives; a whole multiple of a revolution when pulses are missing while the disk turns: 2 to 5 revolutions (about 400 to 1000) after a recalibration or a long seek at 6 ms per step on a mechanism that withholds its index pulses while the head steps (the model below); any value from a few hundred milliseconds on after a disk was taken out and put back with the motor on |
| `0xA3` | `CNT_RDY_STALE` | 16-bit saturating counter: falls of READY while the motor-on level stays high. The controller lowers READY together with that level with the motor request, on reset and on disable, so only the fall by the rule of 500 ms of settled time without an index edge counts |
| `0xA4` | `CNT_TOL_HIT` | 16-bit saturating counter of `tol_hit_i`: marks the decoder accepted on a train of two. Every `FE` accepted on a pair counts, also one whose ID then has a bad CRC and is not reported (it counts here and not in `CNT_IDDEC`), and every `FB` or `F8` on a pair that opened a data field |
| `0xA5` | `RD_CTRL` | readable and writable; bit 0 high switches the decoder's tolerance off (`dec_tol_off_o`); the decoder consults it at its mark decision, so a change acts from the next mark on, and a field opened before it ends under the rule that opened it; bits 15 to 1 read 0; `0x0000` after reset |

`VERSION` reads `0x0AFE`. The four words derive from inputs the device already had (`st_motor_on_i`, `diag_index_qual_i`, `st_media_ready_i`) and from `tol_hit_i`; for them no port of the device changes and no line of `main.vhd`. The read multiplexer gets four flat register entries and no arithmetic. All four words are cleared only by the device's reset, which is the QNICE reset (`c64_rst_sd_i`): a power cycle, or the reset button held for more than 1.5 s. A short press resets the C64 core only; it clears none of the words and leaves `RD_CTRL` and `WR_CTRL` as they are. The dump command for the words of maps v8 to v10 is `MD 7080 70A5`; `doc/dev-fdd/tools/diag_dump_decode.py` decodes the words of map v10 and decodes dumps of maps v8 and v9 as before. The reference for the user is `doc/1581_dd_debug_device.md`, sections 2.4 and 3.6.

What `IDX_GAP_MAX_MS` reads on a mechanism that withholds its index pulses while the head steps (`doc/dev-fdd/tools/idx_gap_model.py`). The model: the disk turns at a constant speed (199.35 ms per revolution, the tester's R6); no index pulse from the first step pulse of a train until the hold time after its last one (0 or 18 ms; the known hold time is 15.8 to 17.9 ms); the recalibration of the ROM is a Restore of c steps and, 72.2 ms after the end of its BUSY (78.2 ms after its last step pulse), a Seek of 39 steps, both at 6 ms per step (the timeline of section 7); every index phase in steps of 0.1 ms. The Seek alone withholds the pulses for 228 ms plus the hold time, more than a revolution, so a recalibration reads at least two revolutions on such a mechanism:

| Sequence at 6 ms per step, hold 18 ms | Readings |
|---|---|
| recalibration from cylinder 39 | 398 ms (30 % of the index phases), 598 ms (23 %), 797 ms (47 %) |
| recalibration from cylinder 79 | 598 ms (30 %), 797 ms (3 %), 996 ms (67 %) |
| recalibration from cylinder 0 | 398 ms (77 %), 598 ms (23 %) |
| recalibration from any cylinder | 2 to 5 revolutions; 4 from cylinder 14 on, 5 from cylinder 47 on |
| seek over 79 cylinders | 598 ms (56 %), 797 ms (44 %) |

Hold 0 ms gives the same sets of readings with other shares. Hold times of more than about 23 ms after the last step pulse add a sixth revolution (1196 ms) in a few index phases of a Restore from cylinder 79. At the 3.03 ms of WIP-V6-A25 the same recalibration from cylinder 39 read 199, 398 or 598 ms. `diag_dump_decode.py` reads the gap against the index period of the same dump, names a gap within 0.1 of 2 to 5 revolutions as withheld pulses, flags one that is not near a whole number of revolutions, and prints the cross-check with `CNT_RDY_STALE` and `CNT_CHANGE`: a disk that was out for more than half a second with the motor on counts in the first, every latched disk change in the second, and withheld pulses in neither.

Lost sync bytes in the counters of map v6, with the tolerance: `CNT_A1_CAND - 3 * CNT_A1_TRAIN` still counts them, two per record and revolution, whether the tolerance recovered the record or not; `CNT_TOL_HIT` tells how many were recovered; `CNT_IDDEC - CNT_MARK_FE` is about the number of recovered IDs (in `tb_flux_ctl`: 18 - 16 = 2, with 2 uses).

## 6. Found on the way

* **The rate table of the image path wraps.** `step_rate_clk` of `fdc1772.v` is 16 bits wide: the 12 ms entry gives 30,463 ticks, 3.81 ms (checked with Icarus Verilog), and the 20 ms and 30 ms entries of a WD1770 or WD1773 wrap as well. It matters only for a disk image and only if the ROM's probe of the CIA TOD clock fails, which it does not in the core. Not changed: the image path is outside of these changes; the physical path has a table of its own (D6).
* **The Restore bound sets the seek error without the V flag.** A Restore that has not found track 0 after 255 steps ends with the seek error bit, in both paths; the datasheet sets it only "providing the V flag is set", and the ROM's `$08` has V = 0. It concerns only a mechanism whose track 0 sensor never answers. Not changed.
* **A Force Interrupt in the image path.** There `step_rate_cnt` survives a Force Interrupt and delays every following command, of any type, for the rest of the rate time, and `seek_state` survives too. The 1581 ROM sends a Force Interrupt only at `$C367` (initialization) and at `$CADC` (from `$CA84`, the compare loop of a Read Sector, error 7), never during a Type I command, so only custom drive code meets it. Not changed (upstream behavior; for the physical path see D7).
* **A step request can be lost after a Force Interrupt.** The controller's step engine takes a request edge only while it is idle. In the sequence Step In, Force Interrupt 1 ms later, Step Out, the request of the Step Out falls into the recovery of the Step In and is lost, the stale acknowledge of the Step In completes the Step Out, and the head ends one cylinder further in instead of back where it was (case 25h of `tb_fdc1772_physical.sv`, reported by the bench, not checked). It was the same before change 2; the pacing narrows the window to the part of a stepping command's BUSY time in which the controller still works (about 3.03 of 6 ms for code 00, a quarter for code 01, all of it for codes 10 and 11). The ROM never reaches it; custom drive code could. Not changed. Two cures are possible: the controller latches an edge that arrives while its engine works, as `step_pend` does behind a write, or the WD model keeps an "acknowledge outstanding" flag across a Force Interrupt and holds the next request until the stale acknowledge has come.
* **The proofs P-M4 and P-M5 depend on the rotational phase.** In `doc/dev-fdd/rom_emu/run_proofs.py` they run the F011 login. At a single phase origin of the disk model, the Seek of 36 steps before the decisive Read Address commands moves that phase at 6 or 12 ms per step, and the Read Address sequence becomes 4, 7, 8 instead of 5, 10, 11, so both fail. They therefore run over 20 phase origins, 10 ms apart: P-M4 holds when some phase meets the ID of sector 11 and ends with job error 2 before any Read Sector, P-M5 when with the IDs outside 1 to 10 hidden every phase succeeds byte-exact (each is the red control of the other). At 1.5, 6 and 12 ms per step P-M4 finds 1, 2 and 2 such phases and P-M5 20 of 20: no dependence of the DOS on the seek time. `machine.py` keeps 1.5 ms per step and takes `--t1-step-ms` (D20); the write proofs (`run_write_proofs.py`) pass at 1.5, 6 and 12 ms (they rewrite their fixtures, which at another step time come out at another rotational phase; a run at 1.5 ms writes them byte for byte as they are in the repository).
* **The pace of the read presentation is 31.5 µs.** `PHYS_PACE_TICKS` = 252 ticks of `clk8m_en` was derived for a 7.88 MHz clock enable; in the core it is 8.000 MHz (every second tick of the drive's exact 16 MHz clock enable), so a byte is presented every 31.5 µs, 63 cycles of the drive CPU, 1.6 % faster than the disk delivers it (32 µs, 256 ticks). The worst-case latency of the ROM's loop at `$C969` is 34 cycles (`doc/dev-fdd/rom_emu/README.md`, P-T), so the margin is 29 cycles instead of 30. The comments of `fdc1772.v` state the real pace; the value is not changed.
* **The format time of R5 and R6 depends on the phase when it is taken from the first cylinder.** Extrapolated from the first cylinder, from the first Write Track to the third, the format of 80 cylinders includes the wait of the first Write Track for its index pulse, and the Type I commands before it decide how long that wait is. These proofs of `run_257_proofs.py` therefore measure from the second Write Track to the fourth (side 1 of cylinder 0 to side 1 of cylinder 1): every Write Track starts at an index pulse, so that does not depend on the phase at which the format begins. With the index reference shifted by 0, 50, 100 and 150 ms the measurement from the first cylinder gives 1091.3 to 1243.3 ms with the read-ahead and 4691.1 to 4843.1 ms with delivery v2 alone, the one from the second Write Track 1200.0 ms and 4800.0 ms every time, with the steps at the rate and at the acknowledge alike (16 runs of the format scenario of R5 and R6 with the index reference `origin` of the disk model shifted, an experiment outside of the repository). R5 and R6 print 96.0 s for a real chip and for the read-ahead and 384.0 s for delivery v2 alone with the steps at the acknowledge, the hardware of comment 17 (measured there: 403 s).
* **`pllcheck.py` misses the weak header at its default.** With its default phase gain of 0.60 the PLL of `doc/dev-fdd/tools/part_e/format_stop/pllcheck.py` does not read the ID of sector 7 in any revolution; with 0.45 and 0.30 (`--phase`) it reads it. The gained data fields are confirmed with all three gains. The survey accepts a confirmation at any of the three gains and runs the two lower ones only on the sides where a variant used its tolerance or saw a train of two: gains chosen on the case, so this confirmation is weaker than it looks. The good ID and data CRCs in all three revolutions and the Greaseweazle software's own conversion of the separate D81 read, which reported 10 of 10 sectors for that side, carry the evidence. The analysis, section 4, says so.
* **Two details of the model of the decoder.** `decmodel.py` let a gap grow beyond 65,535 cycles, where the counter of `physical_1581_mfm_gaps.vhd` saturates, and cleared its byte framing on a gap error, which `physical_1581_mfm_bits_to_bytes.vhd` keeps. Both are corrected. The first showed only on flux-free stretches (6 of 4,234 gap errors on G1, cylinder 81, head 1, with a length of 110,360 against 65,535 cycles, and no decision); the second cannot change a mark, because after a gap error the parser is idle and no byte counts before the next candidate re-aligns the framing. The survey therefore stands; `rtlcmp.py` agrees on that side and on the failing side, with and without the tolerance.
* **The index period of `ctl_run.log`.** The log of `tb_flux_ctl` was made with `G_REV_CYC = 9954565`, the mean revolution of the looped flux; the default of the bench, 9954550, shifts the index edges by 15 cycles per revolution. The command in `doc/dev-fdd/tools/part_e/README.md` names the value.
* **A loss of lock does not disarm the data mark.** A gap error does not clear `data_armed` of the decoder. It is older than these changes and harmless for the tolerance, whose data mark needs the zero-run window as well. Not changed. No unit scenario combines a weak first sync byte with a gap error in between.
* **`G_SETTLE_CYC = 0`.** With that value the head never counts as settled after a step: reads wait for ever, and READY can no longer fall after a step. It was so before change 1; no configuration and no bench uses 0. The controller rejects the value with an assertion at elaboration (`severity failure`, as `mega65.vhd` and `avm_cache.vhd` check their generics).
* **The rule of WIP-V6-A25 failed outside of `UI` too.** On the genuine ROM with a mechanism that withholds its index pulses (hold 18 ms): at its own pace silently at power-on (at 4 of 8 times of the first index pulse) and after `UJ` (3 of 8), where the job error 3 falls inside the initialization, which ends without a message; with the pace of change 2 also at the bump after read errors, where the DOS then reports `74,DRIVE NOT READY` instead of `23,READ ERROR`, and in a write job after a seek of 78 cylinders. The rule of change 1 passes every one of them (section 8.1).

## 8. Proofs

### 8.1 READY while the head moves

* **`run_tb_media_contract.sh`** (`CORE/vhdl/test/tb_physical_1581_controller/`): `RESULT: PASS`. * **The bench** `tb_physical_1581_media_contract`. ## 9. What the Vivado build and the hardware test need to show

Vivado (R3 and R6 at least):

* Timing is met in every clock domain. The changes are in the 50 MHz domain (the QNICE clock: the controller, the decoder, the diagnostic device) and in the core clock domain (`fdc1772.v`: a 20-bit counter and its compare).
