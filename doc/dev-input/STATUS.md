## 1. Decisions

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | "Port 1" and "Port 2" in the two submenus are the sockets of the MEGA65; the setting travels with "Flip joystick ports". | The setting describes the device that is plugged in. |
| D2 | The user selects the device; nothing is detected automatically. | The autodetection of the MEGA65 core has false positives and lets quadrature through to the keyboard before it has decided (mega65-core issues #963, #779, #549). |
| D3 | A new decoder (`amiga_mouse.vhd`) on the raw pins in the core clock domain; the Amiga part of the MEGA65 module `mouse_input.vhdl` stays switched off. | The debouncer swallows the quadrature (the experiment of 2022 on branch `dev-amigamouse` ran through a 5 ms filter, which explains its result); decoding in the core clock domain needs no crossing of a multi-bit position. The Amiga core for the MEGA65 (AExp) reads the same raw lines with success. |
| D4 | The mouse is presented as a 1351: POT value = 64 + position with bit 0 clear; two counts of the mouse are one position unit of a 1351 driver. | Format of a real 1351; scale of the MEGA65 core and of VICE, so the same mouse feels the same in both cores. An exported bit 0 would make the drivers lose half a unit at every call with an odd difference. |
| D5 | Rate limiter: one step per 400 µs, backlog of 127 counts. | A 1351 driver takes 63 or more steps between two calls as a movement in the other direction; fast optical mice would reach that at 8 cm/s. |
| D6 | Right and middle button only for a mouse or adapter that holds pins 9 and 5 high while the buttons are released, found out by observation (a value below 128 has been confirmed); a button that is pressed for 5 s is released. No key of the keyboard as a substitute. | The board cannot tell an open pin from a grounded one. The release covers the exchange or the unplugging of such a device; it is 5 s and not the 30 s of the Amiga core because the two buttons are the up and down lines, which C64 port 1 shares with the keyboard. The Amiga core for the MEGA65 (AExp) uses the same mechanism in the field. A substitute key is essential on an Amiga; C64 programs rarely use the right button. |
| D7 | The direction lines of a port in Amiga mode are hidden from the C64. | On port 1 they are in parallel with the keyboard matrix. |
| D8 | Atari paddles: the count of a longer window, halved (factor 2, window of 510 ticks, cycle of 769 ticks). The cycle is only long while a port is set to Atari, and a port in C64 mode is always counted in the same 255 ticks. | A ratio is independent of the board. A 1 MOhm paddle then reads like a 500 kOhm one. With all settings at "C64" the measurement is the same as before, and a 1351 on a port in C64 mode always sees its window. |
| D9 | The four paddle values cross into the core clock domain through `cdc_stable` instead of per-bit synchronizers. | Found during the analysis: a read of the SID register in the clock cycle of a change could return a value that was never measured (a 1351 jumps by up to 32 units). |
| D10 | Menu: section "Input Devices" in "Advanced Settings" with the submenus "Keyboard Shortcuts", "Mouse" and "Paddles"; the two new submenus have a headline, the two items of port 1, a line, the two items of port 2, a line and "Back"; no `%s` summary on the openers. | Layout asked for by the owner; the line between the ports is an addition. |
| D11 | The 1 ms debouncer stays as it is for the setting "C64". | A 1351 only sends its buttons over the debounced lines; the devices that the filter breaks (section 6 of `RESEARCH-amiga-mouse.md`) also need the core to drive the port lines, which it does not do. |
| D12 | The four comparator inputs of the paddle measurement get a two-stage synchronizer. | Found during the work on the Atari mode: they went straight from the pins into the count enables of multi-bit counters. The measurement moves by 40 ns, far less than one count of 1 µs. |
| D13 | No change of the timing constraints. | The new crossing of D9 sits on a clock pair that `CORE/CORE.xdc` declares a false path, so it is not timed (section 8). A constraint needs a Vivado run to be validated, which was not available. |

No other constant moved.

Amiga mouse at the core clock of 31,527,778 Hz: glitch filter 63 clock cycles (a pulse of up to 63 periods never passes, a level of 65 always does), one step per 12,610 cycles (399.96 µs), confirmation of a button 10 steps (4.0 to 4.4 ms), release of a stuck button after 12,500 steps (5.0 s). At 2,400 counts per second the backlog holds 2 counts at most and the position follows within 0.53 ms. A PAL frame sees at most 50 steps and an NTSC frame 44; a 1351 driver misreads from 63 on. In NTSC mode all times are 3.7 % shorter.

Paddles: with t the time from the end of the discharge to the change of the comparator output, a port in C64 mode reads `min(255, max(0, floor((t + 40 ns) / 1 µs) - 1))` and a port in Atari mode half of the same expression with 510 instead of 255, rounded down. A short cycle has 514 ticks of 1 µs, a long one 769.

## 8. Open points

* **Timing of the paddle crossing.** `CORE/CORE.xdc` declares the QNICE clock and the core clock a false path in both directions, which outranks the `set_max_delay` that `M2M/common.xdc` attaches to every `cdc_stable`. The 32 paths of `i_qnice2main_paddles` are therefore not timed (neither were the per-bit synchronizers before). `cdc_stable` delivers consistent values as long as the 32 bits arrive within one core clock period (30 ns) of each other, which the routes of a small bus satisfy by a wide margin. A `set_bus_skew` on these paths, or a narrower false path, would make that a checked property; it needs a Vivado run to validate.
* **A key as a substitute for the right mouse button**, as the Amiga core has it, is not implemented (D6).
* **USB adapters with a polled wheel protocol** (Micro Tom) may make the pointer twitch, because every discharge of the paddle pins looks like a request to them; the Amiga core has the same effect and the cure (wire 5 cut).
* **A 1351 on one port while the other port is set to "Atari CX30"** sees a measuring cycle of 769 µs instead of 514 µs; by the description of its controller that is harmless, but it is untested.
* **The scale of the Amiga mouse** (two counts per position unit) and the rate limit are generics of `amiga_mouse.vhd`; the hardware test decides whether they feel right.

## 9. Right click with the MEGA key (decided by the owner on 2026-11-17, in work)

The specification is in `SPEC.md`: section 1 (what the user gets), section 5.1 (the key and the rule) and section 8 (menu items, constants and the port `main_mouse_rclick_o`).

* **Behavior:** for a port that is set to "Amiga" and has the switch on, a press of the left button that begins while the MEGA key is held is reported as the right button (the up line of that port) and not as the left one (the fire line stays released). The key at the moment the button goes down decides for the whole press: a swap that followed the key in every clock cycle would end a right click with a left click whenever the key is released a moment before the button. A right button that the device itself delivers (section 4.5 of `SPEC.md`) keeps working in parallel. The C64 still sees the MEGA key as its Commodore key. The switch has no effect on a port that is set to "C64".
* **Menu:** two single-select items in the submenu "Mouse", off by default, each directly behind the item "Port n: Amiga" of its port and visible only while that item is selected: ` #1: MEGA+left = right` and ` #2: MEGA+left = right`.
* **Where:** the keyboard driver (`m2m_keyb.vhd`) delivers the state of the MEGA key to the port conditioning (`debouncer.vhd`); both are in `framework.vhd` in the core clock domain. The two menu bits travel like `main_mouse_amiga_o`.
* **Budgets:** the menu gets 325 lines and `OPTM_ITEMS` 3,450 characters. The new items are the lines 302 and 306, and the constants behind line 301 move: `C_MENU_MOUSE_P2_AMIGA` to 305, `C_MENU_PADDLES_P1_ATARI` to 313 and `C_MENU_PADDLES_P2_ATARI` to 316. The menu heap needs 4,772 + 702 = 5,474 words; `menu_test.py` asks for 180 free words at peak, so `MENU_HEAP_SIZE` grows from 5,632 to 5,696 words (222 free) and `HEAP_SIZE` shrinks to 24,512 (release) and 1,472 (debug build). The deepest stack chain is expected to go from 697 to 701 of 768 words; the full-system simulation measures it.
* **Documentation for users:** the owner had three issues opened in the documentation repository `Kugelblitz360/C64MEGA65DOCS`: #39 (Amiga mouse, with the two switches), #40 (Atari CX30 paddles) and #41 (a 1351 in one port while the other port is set to "Atari CX30": untested, a temporary warning). They announce a comment for the case that the implementation or the hardware test changes what they say: the labels, the behavior, the five seconds after unplugging, the result of the test with the 1351.
* **Agreed with the owner, to be done after his first test build of the branch:** a `set_bus_skew` of 10 ns for the 32 paths of `i_qnice2main_paddles` as a commit of its own, with `report_bus_skew` in the build script and a check in `signoff_gates.tcl` (a violation does not show in WNS or WHS and does not fail a build by itself); read `signoff_gates.tcl` first for gates that react to a new constraint. He then builds once more to test it.

## 10. How the work was organized

A controller wrote the specification from two research reports (all links of both issues, the sources of the MEGA65 core and of the Amiga core, the board schematics) and from the code, and one implementing agent per part worked on a private clone: menu and firmware, the Amiga mouse with the wiring, the paddle measurement, the program for the hardware test, and the full-system simulation. Every design change was then reviewed by an independent agent on a clone of its own, which got the specification and the commits and nobody's conclusions, wrote mutants of its own and reported; its findings went back to the implementing agent. The controller integrated the commits and ran all suites on the integrated tree.
